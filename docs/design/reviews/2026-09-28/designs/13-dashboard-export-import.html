Exporting and importing a dashboard — one file, the dependencies you choose, never a secret

None of this is built. R43 asks for it and F38's criteria define it (AC-354 … AC-359). Export offers what the dashboard depends on: the library charts it shows, the models its charts use, and the data sources those models bind to. Each is named with what uses it, and only those you may see are offered. The dashboard's own charts always go, and bookmarks never do. A data source goes as its driver, settings and description, never a secret, sealed or not. The result is one file.

Import (redrawn 2026-09-28 to your simplified workflow) always puts what the file holds into your My workspace as new drafts, with a target folder you choose. It never replaces, renames or skips anything shared. From there, each draft follows the usual flow: it reaches a folder only when you propose it and the proposal is approved (designs 04 and 05). Before Import, the pane warns, without blocking, about what the drafts may affect once proposed:

  • a same-named item in the target or in your workspace;
  • a cube name the target already has.

You map or create each data source, and a created one is tested before it's saved. A data source whose name the target already holds must be mapped to that one, or created under another name (AC-282); that is the one thing that blocks. With sign-in off, the import still goes to My workspace, and the single user approves their own proposals. A file it can't read, or one holding something this wechart doesn't have, is refused, naming the part and why, and nothing changes. Export is unchanged.

Both halves use design 01's docked pane; the table under the first notes says why. Screen 1 is Wren Writer exporting Revenue overview from Sales / EMEA. Screen 2 imports that file into a second wechart, where:

  • Sales / EMEA already has a Revenue overview (version 4), a sales.yml (version 2), and a customers.yml defining cube customers;
  • Sales has sales_dw, but there's no emea_replica;
  • Wren has a draft Top products in My workspace.

Importing models waits on workspace models (T347) and on models going with a dashboard's proposal (T350). Model rows are drawn as the design intends and tagged Build: needs T347; state V shows the import with models switched off.

Export: what changes, and why

One pane answers the three things an exporter needs: what will go, what each thing is used by, and what will never go. Every choice R43 gives is a checkbox you can see, and everything outside your choice (the dashboard's own charts, bookmarks, secrets, what you can't see) is said in words rather than left out quietly.

Dialog or pane? Decided on the evidence: the docked pane, for both halves

The closest approved precedent for Export is a dialog: Edit in my workspace (design 04, state D) chooses a dashboard's models and data sources by checkbox in 520 px. The case for the pane is the stronger one.

Question520 px dialog (Edit in my workspace, Propose)480 px docked pane (this design)
What's in itEdit in my workspace lists a few boxes with no "used by" lines. Export lists every dependency with what uses it, in three groups, plus what isn't offered and the file's summary. That grows with the dashboard, and a 520 px dialog would scroll inside itself, as the old wizard did (UX-201).Full height, 852 px. The head and foot are pinned, and only the tree scrolls.
StepsFine for one step. Import has four, one of them a data-source form with a connection test: design 01's measured case, where the dialog moved and resized at every step (UX-213).The same frame at every step, as in design 01 (approved) and design 11 (approved, R35).
ContextHides the dashboard, so "Used by 5 charts" is only words.Export keeps the dashboard in view (and proposal 12 outlines the tiles). Import keeps My workspace in view, where a same-named draft of your own may be.
Where it belongs—references.md › Phase 2: what belongs to an item opens beside it (Access, History). Export belongs to the dashboard.
Losing workExport has nothing to lose. Import holds typed secrets and choices.Export closes on Esc at once. Import asks "Discard this import?" on Esc, × or Cancel once a file is chosen (state X, rule 85).

Import: what changes, and why

Redrawn to your simplified workflow of 2026-09-28. An import always lands in My workspace as new drafts, and each draft then follows the usual flow: a target, then Propose, then Approve (designs 04 and 05). The pane no longer asks what to do with each same-named item. Before Import, it warns about what the drafts may affect once they're proposed, and never blocks. Nothing is written until the last step, so leaving at any point, or being refused, changes nothing.

The proposals add something R43 and AC-354 … AC-359 don't ask for; each is marked where it's drawn. The questions are gaps or conflicts in the requirements. Each is drawn one way so the design is complete, and the way drawn is only a default until you decide.
